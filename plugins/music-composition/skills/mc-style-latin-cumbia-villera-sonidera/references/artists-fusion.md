# Regional cumbia specialization: Mexican sonidera and Argentine cumbia villera: artist and fusion case-study prompts

**Interpretation:** experimental production exercises rather than claims about exact songs. **Region:** Mexico and Argentina.

## Historical starting point
Mexican sonidera often foregrounds DJ/MC identity, keyboard/accordion motives and a looping dance bass; cumbia villera has distinct keyboard, bass and vocal storytelling. Treat both as regional cumbia, not Colombian folklore reenactments.

## Contemporary transformation
Write a strong repeating keyboard/accordion melody, a back-and-forth bass motive, and controlled synth/pad entrances. In villera, separate spoken vocal interaction from sung hook. In sonidera, leave room for DJ identification/shouts where stylistically appropriate.

## Evidence discipline
Seek original recordings, interviews, credited personnel, ethnomusicology, and first-party historical archives before claiming track-level notes, tempos or musician credits. Avoid extrapolating every performer into a 'genre default'.

## Tests
1. Remove the traditional instrument/role and see if the arrangement identity collapses.
2. Compare contemporary overlay active/inactive without changing the melody.
3. Verify spec schema, and that no two parallel layers claim the same dimension.
