# Calypso, soca, reggae, ska, dancehall and Caribbean crossover: artist and fusion case-study prompts

**Interpretation:** experimental production exercises rather than claims about exact songs. **Region:** Trinidad and Tobago, Jamaica and Caribbean diaspora.

## Historical starting point
Separate calypso/soca from Jamaican ska/reggae/dancehall. One-drop reggae is not universal reggae; ska offbeat guitar and soca's festival drive have different instrumental engines.

## Contemporary transformation
Soca can prioritize four-to-floor dance energy and chanted hook; dancehall may prioritize riddim and deejay phrasing; reggae can use one-drop, rockers or steppers as selected substyles. Avoid claiming all Caribbean music is Latin, while retaining this as an opt-in fusion source.

## Evidence discipline
Seek original recordings, interviews, credited personnel, ethnomusicology, and first-party historical archives before claiming track-level notes, tempos or musician credits. Avoid extrapolating every performer into a 'genre default'.

## Tests
1. Remove the traditional instrument/role and see if the arrangement identity collapses.
2. Compare contemporary overlay active/inactive without changing the melody.
3. Verify spec schema, and that no two parallel layers claim the same dimension.
