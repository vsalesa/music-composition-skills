# Salsa: artist-led evolution and fusion notes

## Original and contemporary engines

Classic Cuban son and New York salsa dura involve a specific relationship among son clave, bass tumbao, guajeo/piano montuno, conga, bell/cáscara and chorus. Salsa romántica gives more structural weight to an extended lead melody and pop-type song form; Colombian salsa can foreground exceptionally defined ensemble riffs and rhythmic variations. The chosen branch must be stated in ARR-SPEC's `meta.fusion_note` when combined with another style, or in `form[].note` for single-style decisions.

**Evidence level:** these are general directions for analysis, not measured note transcriptions. Exact rhythmic placements, record credits, BPM and chord voicings require the specific recordings and reliable source.

## Fusion experiments

1. **Salsa + reggaeton:** build a full son-driven coro-pregón section, then hand over the beat with a silence and a shared two-note motif; do not stack two complete percussion loops.
2. **Salsa + R&B:** keep the groove and orchestral distribution; let R&B own voice phrasing and color tones. Ensure the lead does not obscure instrumental responses.
3. **Salsa + jazz:** choose a soloist's harmonic language and ensemble arrangement without erasing the existing dance pulse.
4. **Salsa + pop:** shorten introduction and introduce lead hook earlier; retain recognizable clave-oriented bass, piano and brass roles.
