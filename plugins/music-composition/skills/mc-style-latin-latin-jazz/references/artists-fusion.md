# Afro-Cuban jazz, Brazilian jazz, Latin big band and modern Latin jazz: artist and fusion case-study prompts

**Interpretation:** experimental production exercises rather than claims about exact songs. **Region:** Cuba, Puerto Rico, Brazil and wider Americas.

## Historical starting point
Don't use Latin-jazz as a single rhythm: select Afro-Cuban clave-based approach, Brazilian samba/bossa or another Latin-American rhythm family. Jazz swing may coexist with straight Afro-Latin subdivision only through a planned arrangement.

## Contemporary transformation
Write chord substitutions and improvisation forms while retaining percussion/bass roles. Use brass voicing, rhythmic kicks and a distinct soloist background; piano montuno vs jazz comping must be explicitly assigned per section.

## Evidence discipline
Seek original recordings, interviews, credited personnel, ethnomusicology, and first-party historical archives before claiming track-level notes, tempos or musician credits. Avoid extrapolating every performer into a 'genre default'.

## Tests
1. Remove the traditional instrument/role and see if the arrangement identity collapses.
2. Compare contemporary overlay active/inactive without changing the melody.
3. Verify spec schema, and that no two parallel layers claim the same dimension.
