# Bachata and cumbia · guitar logic, regional grammar, sound authenticity

## Bachata: write for a quartet of musical roles

**Choose traditional Dominican / modern-pop / urban-R&B bachata.** Essential roles: requinto (lead phrases and answers), segunda (rhythmic guitar chord figures), bass (repeating motion/anticipation), bongó and güira (hand-percussion engine). A general nylon-guitar chord preset plus reggaeton percussion is not automatically bachata.

**Guitars:** write **two different parts**. Requinto states a 1–2-bar melodic identity, often higher in register and idiomatically picked/ornamented. Segunda articulates subdivisions and chord voicings, anchoring time while leaving holes for the lead. If both run fast eighth notes in the same register the arrangement is cluttered. Use actual pitched guitar articulations, preferably multi-samples/velocity layers for picking and strums. A cheap synth is an honest draft; do not claim it sounds like a picked requinto.

**Rhythm section:** choose a bongó martillo-like texture with occasional accents/changes and a separate güira scrape gesture. Keep their dynamics different and rhythmic subdivisions interlocked. Bass note motion should support the harmonic cycle, articulate short pickups and not simply duplicate every kick.

**Harmony:** propose 4–8-bar functional pop/romantic structure or more traditional cycles depending on brief; voice lead the segunda. Extended R&B chords are an **overlay choice**, not a replacement for the entire bachata groove. Ensure guitarist's melodic notes target the changing harmony and resolve prepared tensions.

**Form:** instrumental intro (hook identity) → lead vocal/verse or instrumental A → requinto response → chorus/payoff with stronger strum → short melodic variation/break → return. Preserve the principal guitar motif; vary phrase endings when the progression repeats.

**In performance:** short attacks, muted/strummed vs picked notes, occasional slides/grace notes only when available. The guitar note duration and sample envelope matter as much as pitch. Avoid one-sample pitch-shifted "guitar" over multiple octaves.

## Cumbia: never infer one transnational preset

| Branch | Core sound/roles | Compositional priority |
|---|---|---|
| Colombian traditional (varies by region) | Drum/percussion ensemble with gaita/flutes or accordion in some traditions | Preserve actual regional rhythmic engine and melodic call/answer |
| Colombian accordion cumbia | Accordion lead, distinct percussion/bass parts | Bellows phrasing, bass pulse and response |
| Mexican sonidera | Keyboard/accordion/synth lead and bass, prominent dance rhythm | Memorable short keyboard hooks and planned stops |
| Argentine villera | Keyboard and synth bass idiom with MC/vocal function | Repeating riff and distinct drum/keyboard accents |
| Peruvian chicha | Electric guitar leads with psychedelic processing | Guitar phrasing, controlled delay, bass/percussion dialogue |
| Modern electro-cumbia | Deliberately selected regional foundation plus electronic accent | Document which rhythmic identity survives synth replacement |

**Common failure:** call a random reggaeton dembow with accordion "cumbia". The selected branch determines percussion accent families, melody lead and timing. Select branch, then adapt instrumentation; do not mix incompatible traditional drum grammar by default.

**Bass vs lead:** cumbia bass often feels cyclical and spacious relative to a busy lead. Keep bass attacks sufficiently distinct from drum lows. Accordion/gaita or guitar responds in **phrases**, with silence, not a constant 16th-note stream. Harmonically simple is fine if lead motif, groove and form develop.

## Two-bar composition brief

1. Choose a narrow pitch range and create a clearly named **hook owner** (requinto or gaita/accordion/guitar).
2. Fix the appropriate percussion family, tempo convention and bass role; avoid forcing clave if not characteristic.
3. Arrange four bars: hook question, rhythmic response, repeat with altered register, cadence/turnaround.
4. Give acoustic sounds articulation: pluck and slide, scraped/rattled percussion, conga/other drums distinguish stroke families.
5. Make three A/B candidates: (A) faithful genre foreground, (B) developed response over same drums/chords, (C) single fusion overlay while core rhythm remains fixed.

## Studio sound decisions

If no authentic guitar multisample is installed, choose a closer patch only for learning and ask for a licensed guitar sound when a convincing final is required. Avoid installing unknown SynthDef binaries. If the sampled accordion lacks expressive direction, limit lead phrase to short patterns and use silence rather than fake sustain. Downloaded loops should be tempo/key aligned, legally usable and musically editable, not a replacement for the full rhythm section.

Detailed regional choices should be grounded in a reference recording supplied or identified for **study**, with no melody copying.
