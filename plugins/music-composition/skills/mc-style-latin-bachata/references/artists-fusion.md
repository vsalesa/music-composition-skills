# Dominican bachata, electric bachata and urban bachata: artist innovation and fusion study

This is a **compositional analysis guide**, not a transcription, authenticated quotation or voice-cloning instruction. Do not infer track-specific tempos, keys, clave orientations, MIDI notes or recording credits without checking an actual source recording/score or credit.

## Tradition to present
**Geographic lineage:** Dominican Republic / New York diaspora.
**Branches worth comparing:** traditional guitar bachata, electric bachata, bachata moderna, urban / New York bachata, bachata pop.
**Root grammar:** Map lead requinto, segunda rhythm guitar, bass, bongó and güira as distinct parts. Guitar syncopations must stay legible; chorus changes may alter bongó to a brighter rhythmic role.
**Contemporary production axis:** Distinguish picked guitar attacks from vocal sibilants and shaker/güira; remove pads during a guitar response.

## Fusion design exercises
### Experiment 1: R&B
**Intent:** Bachata owns guitar-bass-percussion grammar; R&B can own harmony/vocal timbre.
**A/B:** create version A with only the named base grammar and version B changing exactly one borrowed dimension. Keep the motif and vocal register invariant. Verify that the rhythm/low-end owner remains unambiguous; if it changes, mark a section boundary and annotate the transition.
