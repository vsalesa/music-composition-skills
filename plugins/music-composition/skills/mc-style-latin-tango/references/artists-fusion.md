# Argentine/Uruguayan tango, milonga, nuevo tango and electrotango: artist and fusion case-study prompts

**Interpretation:** experimental production exercises rather than claims about exact songs. **Region:** Argentina and Uruguay.

## Historical starting point
Tango relies on bass marcato, syncopation and dramatic articulation; distinguish milonga from salon tango and 3-3-2 gestures from universal meter rules.

## Contemporary transformation
Write bandoneon and violin contrapuntal answers, piano/bass marcato, phrase rubato and intentional dramatic pauses. For nuevo tango, experiment with layered counterpoint, more complex harmony and modern concert forms. Electrotango uses programmed texture without obliterating tango phrasing.

## Evidence discipline
Seek original recordings, interviews, credited personnel, ethnomusicology, and first-party historical archives before claiming track-level notes, tempos or musician credits. Avoid extrapolating every performer into a 'genre default'.

## Tests
1. Remove the traditional instrument/role and see if the arrangement identity collapses.
2. Compare contemporary overlay active/inactive without changing the melody.
3. Verify spec schema, and that no two parallel layers claim the same dimension.
