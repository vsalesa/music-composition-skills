# Brazil, reggaeton and Dominican dembow · lineage-specific arrangement

This reference is **not** a Latin-American super-preset. Specify national/urban branch before discussing beats. Do not equate Brazilian funk with US funk, Brazilian samba with Cuban son clave, or Dominican dembow with every reggaeton recording.

## Samba, pagode and bossa

**Samba:** separate deep surdo pulse, light articulating pandeiro/caixa/tamborim gestures and harmonic rhythm instruments (often cavaquinho and guitar, depending on ensemble). Think about their **counter-motion and subdivisions**, not a sample-pack checklist. The low pulse can be unevenly weighted; emulate intentional hand performance instead of four identical kicks. Chorus/participatory answer and instrumental links are vital to many samba contexts; choose a precise subgenre.

**Pagode:** identify tantã/repique de mão, cavaquinho/banjo and collective-vocal architecture when appropriate to the branch. The hand-drum dynamics and sharp short string attacks interact. Compose a singable cyclical refrain with answer, then remove/reintroduce voices and strings to create sectional energy. Do not put all strummed chords, bass notes and drums on the same subdivision.

**Bossa nova:** intimate nylon guitar with bass-line motion and lightly syncopated upper chord strokes, subtle percussion if used, low-density melody with long breath. The right guitar voice-leading matters more than a densely quantised samba percussion transplant. Write chord voicings carefully (root can sit in bass while inner voices connect); a 4/4 studio representation may fit but accents should respect musical phrasing. When a melody floats over syncopated comping, protect space rather than chase every drum hit.

**MPB:** an umbrella and artistic ecosystem, NOT one transferable groove. First identify underlying samba, bossa, baião, pop, rock or another actual rhythmic source.

## Forró, baião, xote and piseiro

Traditional baião/forró commonly coordinates **zabumba low/high strokes**, **triangle repeating metallic subdivisions** and an expressive **accordion** line. Select specific air/branch and beat-counting; don't flatten zabumba into generic EDM kick/snare. Accordion plays melodic/answering lines and can sustain or ornament tones; triangle accents must be a musical role, not a shaker slapped on top. Xote is felt differently from a fast baião. In keyboard-driven piseiro, clarify what replaces accordion and how the dance engine is maintained.

## Reggaeton / dembow: why the current generator handles it better

**Reggaeton:** a programmed dance groove family makes tight quantisation, short synth hooks, sparse pad loops and intentional sub-decay plausible. Quality still depends on the *chosen* dembow variant, vocal cadence, swing/anticipation in hats and controlled arrangement. Don't make every section sound identical by keeping exactly the same drum+bass grid throughout. Introduce edits/silences as song events.

**Dominican dembow:** vocal/beat exchange and producer edits may be more front-and-centre than a conventional reggaeton chorus structure. Keep dry transient rhythm, aggressive controlled sub, repeated vocal rhythmic identity and planned mutes. Do not assume a single Puerto Rican reggaeton preset serves every Dominican dembow substyle.

## Brazilian funk (baile funk) and modern fusions

A **family of local beat idioms**, not a single "funk" drum map. Pick a specific variant/era and reference its rhythmic grid before choosing kick/snare samples. Often uses punchy dry drum edits, a highly articulated vocal hook and narrow bass motif; but do not prescribe one kick placement as mandatory. Avoid dressing a generic reggaeton groove with Portuguese-language vocal samples and calling it baile funk.

**Fusion engineering:** one style owns pulse/low end at a time. A Brazilian funk beat may own rhythmic grammar while MPB owns chord colour, or samba might own groove while pop owns vocal form. Document what each contributor actually owns. Change sections or gradually transform, not stack complete foreign percussion patterns.

## Sound decisions

- Bossa: close-picked nylon texture, subtle room, real guitar voicings; don't bury in huge reverb.
- Samba/pagode: distinct surdo depth, pandeiro skin/jingle character, precise short cavaquinho strokes.
- Forró: accordion/reed phrasing and triangle/zabumba differentiated tone families.
- Reggaeton: decay-managed sub/kick, narrow synth motif and dry percussion; built-in synth often better than badly pitched samples.
- Dembow: short impactful events and vocal-like hook timing; don't use copyrighted vocal clips.
- Funk brasileiro: chosen kit sounds with scene-specific transients and deliberate silence; compare at the intended dance tempo.

## Evaluation

Solo bass+primary percussion: is the rhythmic family identifiable? Mute all decorative samples and check whether **rhythm placement** alone distinguishes the branch. Does lead phrase rhythm fit the pulse without becoming identical to it? Do sections change with intention rather than forced 4-bar risers? If a sampled acoustic instrument is central, is its articulation plausible across range? If not, prioritize one upgraded sound and A/B against original.
