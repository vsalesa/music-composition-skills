# Merengue · professional Dominican rhythm-section, melodic and sound direction

**Select one branch before writing**: típico/perico ripiao (accordion, sax, güira, tambora); orquestal (brass/sax choir, piano, bass, tambora/güira); pop/romántico (vocal hook and staged dynamics); merengue de calle/electronic (club low end and programmed texture while preserving Dominican rhythmic grammar). Do not treat all of these as interchangeable. Tempo notation can be counted "in 2" or "in 4"—explicitly settle what one Tidal cycle/bar represents.

## Roles and orchestral function

| Role | Must be audible | Producer decision / frequent failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Güira** | Textured sustained-short scraping gestures with strong metrical frame and lighter internal subdivisions | Scraping samples, different length and attack; a closed-hat loop is an emergency sketch, not ideal instrumentation |
| **Tambora** | Dialogue between rim/stick and open/muted head tones, changing over two-bar phrases | Distinguish wood/rim transient from resonant head; do not use one repeated kick as the whole tambora |
| **Bass** | Propulsive root/fifth or chromatic approaches, short punctuation that anticipates changes | Approaching next chord must resolve accurately; avoid overlapping sub tail with fast tambora/kick |
| **Piano/keyboard** | Agile repeated chord fragments, bright attacks, coherent inversions | Write actual note groups and voice-leading, not a static chord on each beat |
| **Accordion or sax** (típico) | Lead motif, short answer, ornamental turnaround | Register/phrasing/bellows or breath are fundamental; sustained fake-organ patch masks the idiom |
| **Trumpet/sax/trombone** (orquesta) | Harmonised brass punctuation and answering phrases | Brass instruments need breath/rest, articulation and coordinated rhythm; do not stack all horns continuously |
| **Lead/coro** | Memorable, singable phrase and group response | Short rhythmic vocal gestures may be more characteristic than long instrumental scales |

Acoustic sound fidelity matters here more than in a minimalist electronic groove. Prefer a verified loaded `guiro`/`tambora` sample, real accordion multi-sample and brass articulations. Tidal Studio's broad `supertrumpet` or generic woodblock can be an honest sketch, not a promised orchestral sound.

## Compose a *two-bar engine*, not isolated independent loops

The following is a **design grid**, not a universal historical transcription. Count sixteenth positions 0–15 per 4/4 studio bar; each bar must be checked by ear against the chosen branch.

1. **Subdivider:** choose continuous güira with a repeatable strong/weak/scrape-short accent hierarchy, not uniform note velocity. Alternate short/long gestures deliberately.
2. **Tambora:** make two distinct rhythmic voices (rim and head); introduce occasional pickup, open tone or closure only where it serves two-bar direction.
3. **Bass:** align strong bass points with harmonic function but allow offbeat anticipation; the last short approach leads *into* the next root, not into a random scale tone.
4. **Piano:** use inversions that move smoothly, high-mid register to stay clear of bass. Contrast chord stabs with a brief running figure; vary density with vocal foreground.
5. **Lead:** original motif A (bars 1–2), an answer B (bars 3–4), A′ at next arrival. Specify where the melody **does not** play so the ensemble can answer.
6. **Ensemble:** on major section changes, a coordinated pickup/drop may involve tambora, bass, piano and horns together. No machine-gun fill on every fourth bar.

## Típico and orquesta: different arrangements, not palette swaps

**Típico:** accordion serves as primary narrative instrument. Constrain lead to playable phrase lengths and instrument register, with ornamented turns and rests. Sax can shadow, harmonise in short gestures or answer. The güira/tambora interaction and buoyant bass remain foreground identity. Example 16-bar arc: instrumental statement (4) → rephrased lead (4) → answer (4) → response/turnaround (4). Melody must evolve; chord loops can remain compact.

**Orquesta:** brass and sax figures can carry a "mambo" passage that is distinct from the sung coro. Write a *horn rhythm map* first: calls, harmonised answer, dropout, short held approach to next phrase. Use a small family of voice-leading directions (common tone, stepwise movement, contrary motion) and occasional 3rd/6th voicing; avoid every horn doubling one sampled unison note. Stage first exposure sparsely, then full brass entrance at payoff.

**Modern/club:** choose which layer owns the downbeat and which brings Dominican gesture. Add one electronic bass/FX dimension to a still-legible acoustic engine; don't merely superpose 4/4 house kick plus unmodified merengue orchestra. Explicitly decide whether the low sub or acoustic bass leads in each section; shorten tails and use subtraction before transitions.

## Quality-first original melodic construction

- Compose a 1–2-bar motif whose rhythm can be clapped and whose note contour can be sung. A strong motive is recognisable with only two or three notes.
- Use chord targets at structurally strong melodic events: characteristic arrival on root/third/fifth, optional prepared suspension or upper neighbour. Offbeat passing tones may be deliberately more adventurous.
- Plan a question/answer with a **different ending**. In a four-bar I–IV–V7–I turn, treat V7 as a chance to create tension, not automatic random notes from the global major scale.
- Answer the lead in **empty slots**: if horns respond, let the lead have a rest and reduce piano density. A sax or accordion doubling melody exactly throughout is not arrangement.
- Preserve rhythmic identity while altering motif by sequence, displacement, fragmentation or registral lift, not wholesale rewrite for every section.

## Sound direction and performance

**Güira:** at least two contrasting recordings (long scrape and short return) with controllable accent; velocity/timbre difference by role; clip overlapping scrape tails or choke thoughtfully. **Tambora:** distinct rim, open head and muted head; mapped articulation with measured onset, not one arbitrary GM drum. **Accordion:** clean attack, phrase separation and expressive envelopes; pitched multi-samples preferred to transposing one-shot over 30 semitones. **Brass:** vary short vs sustained articulations, allow repeated-note breath; arrange voice ranges, avoid fake permanent triple-octave unison. **Piano:** short release, coherent voicing and human-like strong/weak accents. **Bass:** medium-dry, precise note-off and pickup, not long sub sustain.

When assets are missing, tell Maestro one exact missing articulation and a precise free-license sample query; don't request ten unrelated instruments. Check loaded sound IDs and actual samples before composing "ready-to-play" code.

## Diagnosis by symptom

| It sounds like… | More likely cause | First repair |
|---|---|---|
| Techno with metal scraping | Kick owns all strong beats; tambora has no differentiated pattern | Rebuild two-sided tambora and soften/relocate programmed kick |
| A MIDI demo | Organ-like fake accordion, one-note trumpets, generic snare | Replace *one* style-bearing timbre with correct articulation and A/B |
| Four unrelated patterns | Bass/piano/lead do not share harmonic and rhythmic targets | Write a 4-bar chord + phrase map, redo only one failing part |
| Endless loop | Motif never returns transformed, horns present constantly | Add a deliberate stop/answer and second-section change |
| Generic salsa | Cuban clave or piano montuno copied unchanged | Remove forced clave; restore Dominican tambora/güira grammar |

## Evidence and limits

Berklee's profile of Juan Luis Guerra traces Dominican merengue instrumentation to tambora, güira and accordion, while its arrangement teaching emphasises independent roles and phrasing. Contemporary crossover can add harmonies/modern production without discarding this engine. Treat each quantified microtiming/artist claim as **unverified unless recording/transcription is supplied**.
