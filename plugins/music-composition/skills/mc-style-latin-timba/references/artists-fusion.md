# Cuban songo and timba: artist innovation and fusion study

This is a **compositional analysis guide**, not a transcription, authenticated quotation or voice-cloning instruction. Do not infer track-specific tempos, keys, clave orientations, MIDI notes or recording credits without checking an actual source recording/score or credit.

## Tradition to present
**Geographic lineage:** Cuba.
**Branches worth comparing:** songo, timba, timba-funk, contemporary Cuban funk crossover.
**Root grammar:** Write two-bar clave-aware cycles and explicit gears (marchas): each planned gear alters the bass, piano, bell and kit parts at a coordinated cue. A straight repeated salsa loop does not become timba by adding fills.
**Contemporary production axis:** Drum kit and hand percussion must share a distinct rhythmic division of labor; gear changes should be audible even at constant BPM.

## Artist lenses
| Artist / movement | Historical or stylistic lens | Testable composition experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Juan Formell and Los Van Van | Songo's electric rhythm-section language | Arrange bass, percussion and keyboards as one changing mechanism |
| Changuito | Songo kit independence and cross-rhythm | Allocate bell, snare ghosts and kick roles rather than layering samples |
| José Luis Cortés / NG La Banda | Brass bloques and timba vocabulary | Introduce a sharp horn block as a sectional turning point |
| Alexander Abreu / Havana D'Primera | Modern melodic brass-led timba | Develop trumpet responses between vocal phrases |
| Cimafunk | Afro-Cuban rhythm meeting soul/funk | Let funk timbre transform the song while preserving a selected Cuban rhythmic cell |

## Fusion design exercises
### Experiment 1: funk
**Intent:** Cuban cycle remains the main rhythmic identity; funk adds bass/guitar articulation.
**A/B:** create version A with only the named base grammar and version B changing exactly one borrowed dimension. Keep the motif and vocal register invariant. Verify that the rhythm/low-end owner remains unambiguous; if it changes, mark a section boundary and annotate the transition. 

### Experiment 2: electronic
**Intent:** Use an intentional change of gear and program sounds per-part rather than overlaying an EDM kick blindly.

## Cross-skill operations
1. Read `mc-workflow` §2.4 and select base vs overlay according to musical function, not artist prestige.
2. Write `meta.style_layers` using only schema-defined dimensions. A single named skill cannot own two contradictory grooves simultaneously.
3. Write a two-bar groove/bass map and a four-section energy map. State what disappears before a chorus or genre switch.
4. Check that any programmed drums and acoustic percussion do not occupy identical transients without intention.
5. Make a negative test that removes the genre-defining instrumental/rhythmic role: if the result still feels the same, the hybrid was only cosmetic.
