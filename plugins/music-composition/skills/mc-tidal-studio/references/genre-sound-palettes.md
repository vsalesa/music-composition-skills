# Composition and sound palette by genre

Select the **specific regional/era variant**, not a continent-wide preset. For each line: preserve its musical *function*; choose sound attributes first, only then identify an actually available sample or synth. These are guide rails, not universal rules or exact artist transcriptions. Deep genre rules belong to the corresponding \`mc-style-*\` skill.

| Genre and variants | Core interaction that makes it recognizable | What an ideal sound must do | Typical compositional improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salsa dura / son montuno | Clave-aware bass tumbao + piano guajeo + hand percussion and coro–pregón | Round articulate bass, short bright piano, distinct conga attacks, horn sections with breath and rests | Return moña with altered ending after a solo or coro |
| Salsa romántica | Salsa percussion foundation with broader lead melody | Defined acoustic transients but space for sustained melodic voice/keys | Contrast verse restraint with a sung chorus and later brass answer |
| Cuban songo / timba | Kit, electric bass, keys, percussion and marchas/bloques coordinated | Tight bass/key attacks, clear bells/horns, audible gear changes | Compose a deliberate ensemble gear shift, not random fills |
| Traditional / urban bachata | Requinto answering lead, segunda guitar, bongó, güira and bass | Picked expressive plucks, distinct high scraping groove, short note tails | Develop an original requinto motif with new chorus response; R&B owns only chosen overlay |
| Merengue típico / orquesta | Tambora/güira and accordion/sax or horn arrangements | Crisp metallic subdivision, differentiated drum attacks and lively accordion | Instrumental call and response, not all-brass all the time |
| Reggaeton | Rhythmic vocal hook and a chosen dembow-derived family, separated bass/kick | Tight impact, defined sub decay, a small number of distinct synth accents | A breakdown that subtracts drums before a transformed hook |
| Dominican dembow | Producer-led beat edits and vocal/percussion exchange | Dry editable percussion, short controlled low-frequency tail | Make silence/beat stops part of the hook |
| Puerto Rican bomba | Dancer–lead-drum response rather than a rigid Cuban clave loop | Distinct hand/barrel-drum stroke colours, organic attack dynamics | Pattern changes answer a dance/vocal gesture |
| Plena | Panderetas, güiro, communal vocal response | Separate hand-drum registers, chorus intelligibility | Modern electronic overlay without replacing pandereta roles |
| Colombian cumbia | Bass-percussion cycle with regional melodic lead (gaita/flute/accordion) | Warm syncopated bass, textured guacharaca, expressive melodic attacks | Vocal/accordion or flute call-and-response |
| Mexican sonidera / Argentine villera | Keyboard/accordion bass and DJ/MC-vocal roles by region | Defined syncopated keyboard patch, short bass attacks | Reuse a simple motif with regional vocal break |
| Peruvian chicha / Amazonian cumbia | Electric guitar hook over a cumbia rhythmic engine | Picked guitar with controlled delay/phase and rhythmic bass | Develop phrase and timbre of guitar rather than a generic rock beat |
| Vallenato | Accordion + caja + guacharaca and chosen vallenato air | Accordion bite with expressive dynamics, dry caja, differentiated scraping | Respond to narrative phrases with a motif |
| Ranchera / mariachi | Actual meter, sung cadences and strings/trumpet/vihuela/guitarrón roles | Warm plucked bass and sharp strum, expressive horn and string swells | Subtract for held vocal line, then return with orchestral answer |
| Norteño / banda / sierreño | Choose accordion+bajo sexto, or tuba/brass, or requinto/guitar; don't combine blindly | Register-separated rhythm and lead, distinctive pluck/reed/brass articulation | Change instrumental hook at a storytelling turn |
| Corridos tumbados | Requinto/string riff and regional narrative, possibly trap cadence | Close-picked guitar, coherent bass, optional *selective* urban sub | Reintroduce guitar hook transformed after urban passage |
| Samba / partido alto | Surdo/pandeiro/tamborim/cavaquinho + participatory voice | Defined low surdo vs bright hand-percussion subdivisions, fast dry plucks | Contrasting coro and percussion density |
| Pagode | Tantã, repique de mão, cavaquinho/banjo, collective voice | Rounded drum attack, bright short string chords and room for vocals | Break down to voice + strings before a full return |
| Bossa nova | Nylon-string guitar/vocal counterpoint, gentle samba-based internal movement | Intimate pluck, soft bass attack, clear midrange | Inner-voice motion rather than busier drums |
| MPB / tropicalismo | Specify regional rhythmic and harmonic source; MPB isn't one beat | Characterful acoustic/electronic combination based on chosen lineage | Transform a motif timbrally without losing its identity |
| Funk brasileiro | Brazilian funk beat family, tight sub and direct rhythmic voice | Programmed fast attacks, dry vocal/synth edits, controlled bass | Strategic cuts and beat–voice handoffs |
| Baião / forró / xote / piseiro | Zabumba/triangle/accordion or keyboard-led modern variant | Deep low drum distinct from bright metallic subdivisions, melodic accordion articulation | Answer original vocal motif with instrumental variation |
| Tango / milonga / nuevo tango | Bandoneon/violin counterpoint, marcato and dynamic pauses | Reed/violin swell, controlled bass pulse, expressive rests | Dramatic stop and reharmonized/altered return |
| Bolero / Latin romantic pop | Vocal phrase architecture, measured chord movement, intimate response | Guitar/piano attacks with short tails and orchestral restraint | A harmonic turnaround before final refrain |
| Latin jazz | Declare actual Afro-Cuban/Brazilian/other rhythm plus jazz interaction | Articulate comping/solo timbres and percussion separation | Solo builds over a real rhythmic foundation, not generic swing |
| Andean huayno | Chosen regional string/wind roles and authentic dance phrasing | Light charango-like pluck, breathy quena and distinct register | Melodic variation and alternating instrumental responses |
| Afro-Peruvian festejo / landó | Cajón, bass/guitar relations chosen for specific tradition | Wooden cajón tones and clear responsive guitar | Distinct percussion/vocal call-and-response |

## Non-Latin genres

| Style | Musical success criterion | Instrument/synth target |
|---|---|---|
| House / techno | Strong kick–bass relationship, motif development, drop/subtraction | Tight electronic kick, one effective bass synth, evolving filter/texture |
| Drum & bass | Break articulation, sub architecture and phrases larger than one bar | Transient-rich break, controlled long sub, contrast in 8/16-bar groups |
| Hip-hop / trap | Rap phrase space and effective negative space | Crisp kick/snare, sub/808 not masking vocal, sparse memorable sample/synth |
| Jazz | Chord voice-leading, groove agreement and improvisational trajectory | Articulate bass, functional keys/guitar comping, idiomatic solo instrument |
| R&B / city pop | Syncopated bass and rich but purposeful voice leading | Warm electric-style keys, articulate bass, airy but restrained synthesizers |
| Rock / funk | Guitar/bass/kit ensemble interaction, dynamics, hooks | Rhythmic guitar attack, expressive bass and distinct drum hits |
| Ambient / cinematic | Identity through motif and timbral narrative, pacing and tension | Long-envelope evolving pad, specific solo hook, staged density/low end |

## Sound-resource reality check

A patch name or sample label is **not** proof of acoustic authenticity. If a style-defining instrument is unavailable:
1. Propose a convincing existing approximation *only if* the motif and articulation still work.
2. Recommend one precise compatible free-license sample with fallback when acoustic attack is indispensable.
3. For repeated pitched playing, prefer suitable multi-sample mapping or a real synth with controlled pitch/envelopes over a single stretched one-shot when that sounds unnatural.
4. Never claim that generic synthetic trumpet, guitar, bandoneon, brass or cajón is indistinguishable from a real performance.
5. A/B the **instrument** and **composition** separately: do not confuse a better sample with a better musical phrase.

## Fusion and modern producer influences

Do not combine complete rhythms independently. Define ownership (groove, low end, timbre, harmony, voice, melodic hook and form) and select one of: **superposition**, **section alternation**, or **gradual transformation**. Try A/B where the composition's motif is preserved but the borrowed genre's timbre/harmony/rhythm enters or leaves. For artist-led ideas use historical contribution + *two concrete arrangement changes*; never reproduce protected melody, recognizable singer voice or a sampled master without rights.
