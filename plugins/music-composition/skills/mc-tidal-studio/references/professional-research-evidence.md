# Professional research and source hierarchy

This library is a **creative, original arrangement guide**, not a transcription repository. The goal is credible genre knowledge without hallucinated measured values or unauthorized reuse of sound masters. Preserve citations and license distinctions.

## Evidence tiers

**A. First-hand / formal:** named musicians' published methods, music-school pedagogy, authorized teaching texts, scholarly groove analyses, original arrangement notes. Use to ground key instrument roles, clave structure, language and historical lineages. Do not copy long charts or proprietary recordings verbatim into skill files.

**B. Recorded performances / user-authorized MIDI:** analyze note onsets, velocity, bar counts, harmonic targets, articulation and sectional arrangement. Give sample size, playing context and whether playback count is in 2 or in 4. Derive *style features*, not exact reproductions.

**C. Community project notes and agent skills:** useful for testing protocols, orchestration architecture, sound-system troubleshooting and systematic revisions, but not automatically expert evidence about Dominican, Cuban or Brazilian performance.

**D. Assertions without verifiable source:** treat as hypotheses. Avoid precise milliseconds, percent of compases, universal genre "rules" or artist exact patches inferred from a broad genre tag.

## Current Tidal Studio corpus caveat

As inspected in November 2026, `server/data/style_profiles.json` declares **three merengue songs** (399 analyzed bass bars), seven salsa songs and nine cumbia songs. These counts are useful **illustrations**, not statistically representative global genre definitions. File tempos, MIDI naming, unknown source performance and doubled/halved beat counting may bias summary. For serious genre coverage build a documented, license-safe evaluation corpus with variant labels, annotations, real percussion articulation and human listening feedback.

A future dataset entry should record: exact regional branch, ensemble type, era, BPM/counting, section, instruments, beat grid, harmonic sequence, sample provenance/licensing, annotation reviewer, confidence level and agreement with other reviewers. Do not mix unrelated variants into a single median pattern then declare it universal.

## Responsible style transfer

Explain **musical mechanisms**, not complete protected passages: identify roles, beat directions, register spans, motif development, arrangement density and production decisions. Do not use external copyrighted tracks or vocals in distributed samples without appropriate rights. For an artist reference, describe *two independent transfer choices* and compose new pitches/rhythms/form.

This reference is a curated source map and critical method, not an exhaustive systematic review.
