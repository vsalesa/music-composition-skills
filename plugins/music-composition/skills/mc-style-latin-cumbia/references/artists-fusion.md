# Cumbia: Colombian roots and Latin American transformations: artist innovation and fusion study

This is a **compositional analysis guide**, not a transcription, authenticated quotation or voice-cloning instruction. Do not infer track-specific tempos, keys, clave orientations, MIDI notes or recording credits without checking an actual source recording/score or credit.

## Tradition to present
**Geographic lineage:** Colombia, Mexico, Peru, Argentina and beyond.
**Branches worth comparing:** Colombian traditional/orchestral, Mexican sonidera, rebajada, cumbia villera, Peruvian chicha/amazónica, Argentine cumbia, electro-cumbia.
**Root grammar:** Different regional cumbia families use different percussion patterns; never force Afro-Cuban clave as a global rule. Map bass cycle, drum or guacharaca/güiro accents and subdivision pattern from chosen branch.
**Contemporary production axis:** For chicha/amazónica preserve guitar picking and effects as a structural hook; for electro-cumbia preserve the bass-percussion cycle rather than applying generic EDM beats.

## Fusion design exercises
### Experiment 1: electronic
**Intent:** Cumbia bass and percussion own groove; electronic layer owns timbre and transitions.
**A/B:** create version A with only the named base grammar and version B changing exactly one borrowed dimension. Keep the motif and vocal register invariant. Verify that the rhythm/low-end owner remains unambiguous; if it changes, mark a section boundary and annotate the transition.
