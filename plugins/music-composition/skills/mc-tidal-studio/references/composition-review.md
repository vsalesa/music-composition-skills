# Musical quality: diagnosis, A/B revision and teaching

Musical excellence is primarily a **composition and arrangement problem**, not a question of maximizing track count, theoretical complexity or sample fidelity. This reference is a critical review rubric and set of practical revision prompts for Maestro.

## Internal quality audit: eight observable questions

Assess **identity, hook, harmony, groove, instrument roles, development, timbre and originality**. For each: weak / acceptable / distinctive. This is **an editorial checklist**, not an objective numerical score of audio quality.

| Dimension | Diagnose | Specific repair |
|---|---|---|
| Style identity | Does the named genre have its characteristic *functional* rhythm and instrument roles? | Restore the characteristic bass–percussion/guitar/piano dialogue; choose a regional variant |
| Motif | Can you describe a 1–2-bar shape or memorable repeated rhythmic idea? | Compose an economical motive with characteristic rest/pickup; repeat with altered ending |
| Harmony | Does the progression lead to tension, release or deliberate stasis? | Improve one voice-leading line or shift harmonic rhythm at a form boundary |
| Melody | Does it phrase with rests and contour? Does it relate to the chord tones and groove? | Remove some notes, create an answering phrase, shift register for the payoff |
| Groove | Are the subdivisions, bass and kick mutually supportive? | Shorten low-end tails, remove redundant drums, re-place anticipations |
| Arrangement | Can you tell verse, chorus, drop and outro apart just by parts/dynamics? | Subtract before adding, change instrument owner or harmonic rhythm |
| Timbre | Does chosen sample/synth support attack, articulation and emotional intent? | Replace only *one* unsuitable sound and compare; don't mask poor writing with FX |
| Originality | Is this an original statement rather than a carbon copy of an artist? | Retain abstract technique, transform motif/harmony/structure and performance |

## Three meaningful variation modes

For a creative request where the app enables \`variantes\` (normally 3–4 choices), compare:
1. **Fidelity:** reinforce the genre's most essential rhythmic/lead instrument. Often subtract a part.
2. **Development:** keep the core motif but add a call-response or harmonically motivated second section.
3. **Contrast:** introduce a purposeful bridge/drop, borrowing another genre's timbre/voicing while keeping song identity.
4. **Optional surprise:** a producer experiment that changes arrangement form or rhythmic feel without becoming unrelated music.

Do not make "more reverb", "less reverb" and "slightly different reverb" three variants. If auditioning specific choices, vary as few factors as possible for intelligible A/B, unless the user explicitly requests contrasting complete arrangements. Avoid rewriting the user's favourite tracks wholesale.

## Quality before resources

**Bad melody + expensive synth = still a bad melody.** First decide if the weakness is pitch contour, rhythm, harmony, phrasing, density or instrument choice. If sound selection truly is the bottleneck, define the sound's attack, decay, tone and articulation before browsing. The \`descargar\` interface cannot replace missing compositional purpose.

## Example: a salsa chorus feels weak

- Check whether a sung coro cell can be recognized after the first pass; don't immediately add more horns.
- Keep clave-aware bass and piano interplay; if they clash, repair the 2-bar grammar.
- Compose a short brass moña that answers the coro in the spaces.
- Try A/B: **A** drop piano in the first bar of the coro; **B** introduce a different horn answer after a half-bar rest; **C** switch to a more restrained vocal+conga pickup before all instruments return.
- Explain to a beginner: "Escucha cómo el silencio prepara la entrada"; ask one small experiment.

## Example: modern Brazilian funk + pop

- Select a specific local funk beat grammar, not generic perreo, with a strong repetitive vocal rhythm.
- Choose dry high-impact percussion, clearly bounded sub and a short, recurring synth/vocal cell.
- For pop crossover let melody/harmony change in the chorus, while the base rhythmic identity remains recognizable.
- A/B controlled choices: A preserves beat and changes vocal hook; B preserves hook and subtracts beat before chorus; C changes the synth arrangement but not the kick/bass grammar.

## Listening limitations and honest assessment

No embedded skill can listen to Studio playback or measure groove errors without actual audio/events being supplied. If only code is available, give a predicted audible effect and an objective structural diagnosis. When events/audio exist, review clashes, repetition and timing, but **do not invent** measured frequency, LUFS, transients, artist gear or human performance metadata.

**Acceptance:** original hook, credible genre grammar, meaningful role separation, intentional harmonic/melodic development, contrasting form and a sound palette whose audible function is explained. A single weak area should generate a practical next revision rather than an inflated numerical "quality score".
